# CivicTrust — Design Principles

## 1. Evidence Before Confidence

The platform should make evidence and sources visible rather than asking users to trust an unexplained AI answer.

## 2. Citizen First

Design for ordinary people rather than experts.

Use:

* plain language;
* clear navigation;
* understandable explanations;
* actionable next steps.

## 3. Clarity Over Complexity

Civic information can already be complex.

The interface should reduce unnecessary complexity rather than add to it.

## 4. Neutrality

CivicTrust must remain politically neutral.

The platform should inform users without persuading them towards a political candidate, party or position.

## 5. Transparency

Users should be able to understand:

* where information came from;
* why a source was selected;
* when information was published or checked;
* whether information is verified;
* what remains uncertain.

## 6. Accuracy With Uncertainty

CivicTrust should never create false confidence.

Where evidence is incomplete, conflicting or unavailable, the platform should say so.

## 7. Privacy by Design

Collect the minimum information necessary.

Avoid unnecessarily requesting:

* NIN;
* BVN;
* banking information;
* passwords;
* other sensitive personal information.

Support anonymous reporting where appropriate.

## 8. Accessibility

Design for people with different abilities and different levels of digital familiarity.

Include:

* readable typography;
* sufficient contrast;
* clear forms;
* accessible controls;
* meaningful error messages;
* keyboard-friendly interactions where applicable;
* touch-friendly mobile interfaces.

## 9. Low-Bandwidth First

The platform should remain practical under constrained connectivity.

Avoid unnecessary:

* heavy animations;
* oversized assets;
* complex visual effects;
* interaction patterns that depend on high bandwidth.

## 10. Mobile First

Assume that many users will access CivicTrust on a mobile phone.

Core journeys should therefore work well on small screens.

## 11. Local Relevance

CivicTrust begins with Nigeria and should reflect local civic contexts while remaining extensible to other countries and regions.

## 12. Multilingual Readiness

The product should be architected so that additional languages can be added over time.

Initial future targets include:

* Yoruba;
* Hausa;
* Igbo;
* Nigerian Pidgin.

## 13. Action-Oriented

Information should help users understand what they can legitimately do next.

The platform should avoid overwhelming users with information that does not contribute to their goal.

## 14. Real Functionality Over Appearance

The proof of concept should demonstrate working journeys rather than simply attractive static screens.

## 15. Honest Product Representation

The product should clearly distinguish between:

* verified information;
* user-submitted information;
* demonstration data;
* AI-generated explanations;
* actual external integrations.

CivicTrust should never imply that something happened when it did not.
