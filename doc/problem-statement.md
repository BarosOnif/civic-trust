# CivicTrust — Problem Statement

## The Problem

Civic information is available in many places, but access to information does not always mean access to **reliable understanding**.

Citizens may encounter information through:

* government websites;
* public documents and PDFs;
* official announcements;
* news organisations;
* social media;
* WhatsApp and messaging platforms;
* community groups;
* word of mouth.

This information environment can make it difficult for ordinary people to determine:

* where the right information is;
* which source to trust;
* what a policy or announcement actually means;
* whether a claim is accurate;
* whether information is still current;
* which institution is responsible;
* what action should be taken next.

## Four Core Problems

### 1. Civic Information Is Fragmented

Relevant information can be spread across multiple organisations, websites, documents and communication channels.

A person may have to search several places simply to answer one civic question.

### 2. Information Is Not Always Understandable

Official information can use technical, legal or institutional language that makes it difficult for ordinary citizens to understand what it means in practical terms.

### 3. Verification Is Difficult

Citizens may encounter claims that are:

* accurate;
* inaccurate;
* partially accurate;
* outdated;
* missing important context;
* unsupported by available evidence.

People may not know how to verify these claims themselves.

### 4. Information Does Not Always Lead to Action

Even after finding reliable information, users may still not know:

* who is responsible;
* which process to follow;
* where to report the issue;
* what documents or requirements apply;
* what the appropriate next step is.

## The Result

These challenges can contribute to a cycle:

**Fragmented information
↓
Uncertainty
↓
Misunderstanding
↓
Misinformation
↓
Frustration
↓
Inaction**

## The Opportunity

CivicTrust is designed to bridge this gap through:

**Information → Evidence → Understanding → Action**

Rather than simply giving users an AI-generated answer, CivicTrust aims to make the information, evidence, sources and appropriate next steps more visible and understandable.

## Why This Matters

The objective is not to create another general-purpose chatbot.

The opportunity is to create a **trusted civic-information layer** that helps ordinary people navigate complex public information with greater clarity and confidence while preserving transparency, privacy and neutrality.

## Intended Outcome

CivicTrust aims to make civic information:

* easier to find;
* easier to understand;
* easier to verify;
* easier to act on.
