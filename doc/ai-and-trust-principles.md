# CivicTrust — AI and Trust Principles

## AI Is an Enabling Tool, Not the Authority

CivicTrust uses AI to help people interact with civic information, but AI output is not treated as the source of authority.

AI may assist with:

* natural-language understanding;
* intent classification;
* information retrieval;
* summarisation;
* plain-language explanation;
* claim extraction;
* evidence comparison;
* translation;
* next-step identification.

## Source Priority

Where possible, CivicTrust should prioritise:

1. Official government sources
2. Public institutions
3. Public records and legal sources
4. Research institutions and credible civil-society organisations
5. Reputable media
6. User-submitted information

Source authority, relevance and freshness should all be considered.

## Evidence Visibility

Where an answer or verification result depends on available evidence, users should be able to inspect:

* source organisation;
* document or page title;
* publication date where available;
* relevant evidence or supporting content;
* source classification;
* original source link where available;
* why the source was selected.

## Accuracy Rules

CivicTrust should never:

* fabricate sources;
* fabricate evidence;
* fabricate laws or policies;
* fabricate institutions;
* fabricate statistics;
* fabricate quotations;
* fabricate contact details;
* present assumptions as facts;
* claim an external action was completed when it was not.

## Uncertainty

When the available evidence is insufficient, CivicTrust should communicate that limitation.

Preferred example:

> “I couldn't verify this from the available sources.”

This does not mean the claim is necessarily false. It means the available evidence is insufficient for CivicTrust to make a reliable determination.

## Conflicting Information

When relevant sources disagree, CivicTrust should:

* show the disagreement;
* identify the sources involved;
* explain the difference where possible;
* avoid silently choosing one version while hiding the conflict.

## Claim Verification

The platform should use nuanced verification statuses:

* **SUPPORTED**
* **CONTRADICTED**
* **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**
* **NOT SUPPORTED**
* **OUTDATED**
* **UNVERIFIED**
* **NEEDS CONTEXT**

The platform should avoid reducing every complex civic claim to a simplistic true/false result.

## Freshness

Where publication or update dates are available, CivicTrust should make them visible.

Possible freshness labels include:

* Current
* Recent
* Older
* Outdated
* Unknown

## User-Submitted Information

User-submitted reports should be clearly distinguished from independently verified information.

Submission of a report does not automatically make the information factual.

## Safety Information

CivicTrust must not invent emergency numbers, support organisations, addresses or contact information.

Where emergency or support information is not verified, the platform should say so.

CivicTrust should clearly distinguish between:

* emergency assistance;
* reporting;
* general information;
* support resources.

## Action Integrity

CivicTrust must never imply that a complaint, report or request was sent to a government agency unless a real integration exists and the submission has actually been completed.

Where a report is only stored inside the POC, use wording such as:

> “Your report has been recorded within CivicTrust for demonstration purposes.”

## Political Neutrality

CivicTrust may present factual information about:

* elections;
* public officials;
* candidates;
* parties;
* policies;
* legislation;
* public statements.

However, it must not:

* rank candidates;
* score candidates;
* recommend candidates;
* tell users how to vote;
* generate targeted political persuasion;
* predict election winners.

The purpose is to inform citizens so they can make their own decisions.

## Human Agency

The platform should help users make informed decisions rather than making civic or political decisions for them.
